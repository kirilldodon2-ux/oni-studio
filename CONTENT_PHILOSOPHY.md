# ONI — Content Philosophy

## The Site as Medium

A portfolio holds work at arm's length. The mechanism is neutral — a grid, a list, a
slideshow — and the work is the subject. ONI rejects this arrangement.

The site is an environment. Content moves through it at different densities, different
depths, different durations. A resolved studio engagement and a fragment from an abandoned
experiment are not equivalent objects placed in equivalent slots. They occupy different
territories of the same space and require different spatial treatment.

This is not a metaphor. It has direct architectural consequences for how content is
structured, how it is disclosed, and how it relates to the atmospheric infrastructure
the site is built on.

---

## What ONI Is Becoming

ONI is becoming four things simultaneously, none of which supersedes the others.

**A studio system.** The documented body of the studio's output — resolved works, ongoing
investigations, process evidence. Not a showcase. An honest record.

**A living archive.** A growing body of material that accumulates over time without
needing to be organized by recency. Its logic is association and proximity, not the
calendar. The archive is spatial, not chronological.

**An experimental publishing space.** A venue for authored writing, technical investigations,
and code-as-medium work. The site publishes because publishing is part of how the studio
thinks — not because content serves a growth objective.

**A cinematic technical editorial environment.** The site is itself a form of craft.
The infrastructure, the typography, the motion behavior, the atmospheric conditions —
these are not scaffolding for content. They are part of what is being made.

These four identities are not in tension. They are the same project described at different
scales.

## Content Archetypes

ONI's archive is organized around five archetypes. Each has its own spatial weight, its
own disclosure rhythm, and its own relationship to the atmospheric infrastructure.

### Works

The heaviest archetype. Authored investigations into specific studio engagements — not
case studies in the agency-brochure sense, but documented positions. What was at stake.
What was made. How it behaves. What it means that it exists.

Works are resolved. They are not published until they are.

**Character:** dense, authored, complete  
**Disclosure depth:** maximum — surface entry point + full interior  
**Surface presence:** title, year, domain classification, one defining image  
**Interior treatment:** full spatial page; typography as structure; images as evidence

---

### Process Artifacts

Provisional material — fragments, early outputs, experimental directions that did not
become works but are not irrelevant. Process artifacts are the evidence of thinking:
what was tried, what opened further questions, what was discarded and why.

These are not making-of content. They are not behind-the-scenes material prepared for
an audience. They exist in their own right as records of a working practice.

**Character:** provisional, fragmentary, specific  
**Disclosure depth:** accessible but not promoted  
**Surface presence:** type, date, brief classification (sketch / render / technical / fragment)

---

### Writings

Authored text. Neither blog posts nor academic papers — positioned observations about
process, form, materials, tools, constraints. A writing exists because something needed
to be said with precision that could not be said visually.

Writings are long-form. There are no excerpts, no summaries, no preview cards. You read
the thing or you do not.

**Character:** authored, precise, non-recurring  
**Disclosure depth:** full text or no text  
**Surface presence:** title, year, reading duration — no preview copy

---

### Code Artifacts

Work where code is the medium — not infrastructure, but output. Interactive experiments,
generative systems, technical explorations that produce a result worth encountering. A
code artifact is a made thing. It runs in a browser, but it is not a demo.

**Character:** interactive or visual, technically specific, self-contained  
**Disclosure depth:** the artifact is the entry point; documentation is optional  
**Surface presence:** the live work or an image of its state — no required description

---

### Atmospheric Fragments

The most diffuse archetype. Material that sets conditions rather than delivers information —
a color study, an image that contextualizes without explaining, a statement about
materials. These are the ambient temperature of the archive.

Not every project generates them. They are not branding artifacts. They are residue — the
part of process that does not fit anywhere else but belongs somewhere.

**Character:** ambient, non-explanatory, atmospheric  
**Disclosure:** encountered rather than searched for  
**Surface presence:** minimal — date, medium classification; no title required

---

## Relationships Between Archetypes

The archetypes are not independent channels. They have gravity toward each other:

- A **Work** may generate **Process Artifacts** during its production and a **Writing**
in its aftermath
- A **Code Artifact** may begin as a constraint within a Work and survive independently
after the project ends
- **Atmospheric Fragments** may accumulate across a project's lifespan and only become
legible when grouped retrospectively
- A **Writing** may reference multiple Works without belonging to any single one
- **Process Artifacts** may contradict finished Works — that contradiction is part of
the record, not a problem to resolve

The archive is not organized by content type alone. It is organized by association:
proximity of subject, period, material, concern, or unresolved question. Content finds
its neighbors spatially, not taxonomically.

This means the archive, in its mature form, is a field rather than a directory.

---

## Disclosure Philosophy

Different archetypes have different thresholds of visibility:

**Works** are the primary surface — the reason someone arrives.  
**Writings** are visible to those who stay — they reward depth.  
**Process Artifacts** are accessible but not emphasized — available to the genuinely curious.  
**Code Artifacts** are encountered — via reference within a Work or Writing, or direct link.  
**Atmospheric Fragments** may not be individually indexed — they may be conditions of
other content.

This is a curated gradient of availability. It is not a content hierarchy and it is not
paywall logic. It is a decision about how much of the studio's interior is visible from
the street — and at what depth of engagement it becomes available.

---

## What the Archive Is Not

- Not a blog organized by date, category, and tags
- Not a portfolio grid of client work cards
- Not a content marketing channel or SEO surface
- Not a social layer, a community space, or an engagement mechanism
- Not a lead generation instrument

The archive is an authored environment. Entering it is like entering a room arranged
with intention. It does not continue indefinitely in both directions. It does not surface
recommended content. It does not measure engagement. It holds what was made.

---

## Editorial Standards

All content in the ONI archive is subject to the following:

**Dated.** Every piece carries the year it was made or published. The archive is honest
about time.

**Singular.** Each piece says one thing. It is not a collection of talking points or a
comprehensive overview. If it requires a table of contents, it is probably two things that
have not yet separated.

**Resolved or provisional — with intention.** Content appears either as a finished document
or as an explicitly provisional one. The classification is visible. Nothing is accidentally
incomplete.

**Titled or untitled — with intention.** A title is not required. But the absence of a
title is a choice, not an oversight.

**Not explained by itself.** Content does not carry its own publicity. No "in this piece,
I will argue..." — no key takeaways, no calls to action embedded in prose.

---

## Long-Term Direction

The archive grows slowly and deliberately. There is no publication schedule, no content
calendar, no volume target. Content appears when it exists.

In its mature form, the archive is navigable by association rather than by category. A
visitor reading a Writing discovers a related Work not through a recommendation widget
but through spatial proximity established by the authors who placed them near each other.
The infrastructure supports this without making it explicit.

The site's atmospheric character — its spatial conditions, its typographic register, its
ambient motion — extends coherently into every content page. A visitor should not
experience the archive as a separate section of the site. They should experience it as
a deeper layer of the same environment they arrived in.
